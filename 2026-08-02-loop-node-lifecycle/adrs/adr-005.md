# ADR-005: Waits and approvals — indefinite by default, always visible, escalation authorable; waiting suspends work-bounds

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-08-01

## Context

Durable waits (timers, external events) and human approvals park a run until something happens. Every surveyed product lets parked work rot invisibly and forever: Sim's expiry column exists but is never written; Mastra suspends indefinitely with no sweeper; LangGraph interrupts wait forever and the ecosystem criticizes exactly that. The briefing proposed expiry + escalation from day one as a differentiator. The engine's own philosophy (ADR-004) says long waits are normal — so the differentiator cannot be a default deadline.

A second interaction: the run-level wall-clock budget. If waiting time consumed it, any approval sitting for days would kill the run — contradicting the indefinite-wait default. Temporal has the opposite behavior (pause does not stretch the retry-budget ceiling), which is precisely what we do not want for day-scale loops.

## Decision

1. **No default expiry.** Approvals and durable waits wait indefinitely by default. Nothing terminal happens by omission.
2. **Visibility is the default safeguard.** Every waiting node/run is enumerable with its age and reason on the operator surfaces (list of runs awaiting approval/event, sortable by age). Rot is impossible to miss, even though nothing expires.
3. **Expiry and escalation are authorable per gate/wait.** An author may declare a deadline with an escalation ladder — re-notify, notify a channel (via reactions, ADR-006), and finally route a declared timeout path. Escalation never silently discards work: an expired wait without a declared route surfaces as needing attention.
4. **Waiting suspends work-accounting.** Nodes in paused/waiting/awaiting-approval/quarantined states are excluded from stall and no-progress arithmetic (they are waiting, not failing) while remaining visible in progress reporting. Time spent waiting does not consume the run's wall-clock budget — the clock measures work, not waiting. Token budgets always count when tokens are spent, regardless of state.
5. **Resume admission is governed and loud.** Resume is single-claim (concurrent resumes resolve deterministically: one wins, the other gets an explicit queued/rejected answer); resume payloads are validated against the wait's declared expectation; failed admission after bounded attempts becomes a visible intervention-required condition, never a silent limbo (Sim's admission pattern, adopted).

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Default expiry with generous deadline (original briefing lean)

- **Description**: Every wait born with a days-scale deadline + escalation; infinite waiting is opt-out.
- **Pros**: Most aggressive answer to approval rot; a differentiator against every surveyed product.
- **Cons**: Conflicts with the long-running philosophy; a wrong default deadline on a critical approval creates an incident.
- **Why rejected**: User decision — visibility, not deadlines, is the default safeguard.

### Alternative 2: Default periodic reminder, never terminal

- **Description**: Untouched approvals re-notify automatically on a cadence.
- **Pros**: Fights forgetfulness without ever being terminal.
- **Cons**: Automatic noise nobody asked for; cadence is another wrong-by-default number.
- **Why rejected**: Reminders belong to authored escalation ladders, not defaults.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Nothing dies because a human was slow; nothing rots invisibly either.
- Wall-clock budgets stay meaningful for bounding work while coexisting with week-long approvals.

### Negative

- Operators must actually look at the waiting inventory (or wire escalation); the platform will not nag by default.

### Risks

- "Waiting suspends the work clock" must be defined once and applied uniformly (pause, durable wait, approval, quarantine); any state counted inconsistently reopens the budget-vs-wait conflict. The techspec owns the exact state-to-clock mapping.
