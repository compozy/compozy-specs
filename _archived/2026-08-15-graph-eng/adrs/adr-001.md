# ADR-001: Human interaction model — four declarable interactions on the wait-resume plane

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-08-16

## Context

Loops today support exactly one human interaction: gate approval (`approve | request_changes | reject`) with a durable escalation ladder. The only state-editing path is the schema-validated payload admission on parked `wait` cells (`ResumeNodeWait`). The graph-engineering evidence corpus (`analysis/02_analysis_graph-frameworks.md` §3, `analysis/01_analysis_graph-eng-concepts.md` C12) shows the mature HITL catalog is wider — structured input, edit-before-execute, respond-as-result, and state amendment — and that the state of the art runs all of them over **one** interrupt/resume plane. The loop-ideas briefing (idea 34) left open whether a "run inbox" should be a new plane; its own anti-lesson 7 (v0's two disconnected signal planes) warns against building a second bus.

## Decision

1. Loop authors can declare four human interactions:
   - **Structured input request** — the run parks until a responder supplies an answer; the answer is validated against the node's declared answer shape and becomes the node's output.
   - **Edit-before-execute** — a proposed action pauses for review; the responder may modify its arguments; the action executes with the edited arguments.
   - **Respond-as-result** — the responder's answer substitutes the action's execution entirely and becomes its result.
   - **Amend-before-resume** (restricted) — an operator repair verb, not author grammar: the output of any parked/paused node can be corrected, validated against the node's declared output shape, with actor provenance recorded, before the run resumes.
2. **Responder rules**: human responders by default; the author may opt in agent responders per node; the self-response prohibition (an agent never answers a request originating from a run it started) is always enforced, mirroring the existing `approval_self_denied` rule.
3. **Unanswered requests** follow the shipped approval escalation ladder: reminder → escalation → expiry with an author-declared outcome (alternate route or halt), configured per node.
4. **One plane**: every interaction above extends the shipped wait-resume plane (durable parked cells + validated admission + escalation). No parallel inbox plane is built. This resolves loop-ideas open decision #6.
5. **Bridges**: human requests can notify external channels (via existing bridges) with a deep link back to answer inside CompozyOS. Answering natively inside the bridge is out of scope (recorded in Non-Goals).

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: New run-inbox plane (loop-ideas idea 34 as originally sketched)

- **Description**: a dedicated `loop_inbox` with `signal` and `update` verbs beside the wait plane.
- **Pros**: clean separation of unsolicited signals from declared waits.
- **Cons**: two planes for one job; emitters and listeners split across buses.
- **Why rejected**: v0 anti-lesson 7 is directly on point — the wait-resume plane already implements the `update` verb's hard parts (atomic claim, schema validation, provenance, fencing).

### Alternative 2: Approval-only status quo

- **Description**: keep gates as the only human surface.
- **Pros**: zero new surface.
- **Cons**: authors cannot express "ask a person mid-run", "let a person fix the arguments", or "let a person answer instead" — the three highest-frequency HITL patterns in production literature.
- **Why rejected**: the gap analysis places HITL breadth among the top capability gaps (checklist items 67–76).

### Alternative 3: Free-form state editing (LangGraph `update_state` equivalent)

- **Description**: allow arbitrary writes to any node output at any time.
- **Pros**: maximum flexibility.
- **Cons**: unconstrained mutation of a fenced, append-only cell table; bypasses schema contracts.
- **Why rejected**: amendment is deliberately restricted to parked/paused cells, schema-validated, provenance-recorded — stronger than the state of the art without giving up auditability.

## Consequences

### Positive

- One durable plane carries the full HITL catalog; every interaction is agent-operable and survives restarts by construction.
- Authors gain the three missing interaction types with the escalation semantics they already know.
- Operators gain a uniform repair verb for parked outputs.

### Negative

- The wait-resume plane's semantics grow; its invariants must be re-stated and re-tested as a contract.

### Risks

- Resume-safety hazards (double effects, stale answers) — mitigated by keeping admission atomic, schema-validated, and epoch-fenced, and by spec'ing the safety invariants as a numbered list in Part II.

## Implementation Notes

Stage 2 decides node grammar, verb names, and payload shapes. The interaction catalog and responder/escalation rules above are frozen.
