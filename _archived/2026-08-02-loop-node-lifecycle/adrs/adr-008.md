# ADR-008: Cancel ≠ kill, with an explicit parent-close policy for spawned work

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-08-01

## Context

The engine has one stop verb today: run-level stop transitions to terminal failed. There is no way to stop a node cooperatively (letting cleanup and notifications run) versus immediately, and no policy for what happens to a sub-loop when its parent closes. Temporal's model is the reference: cancel is cooperative and idempotent (cleanup runs), terminate is immediate (no cleanup), and child workflows carry a parent-close policy defaulting to terminate. Temporal also shows the delivery mechanism — cancellation reaches in-flight work through the liveness acknowledgement channel, no second push path. Mastra provides the anti-lesson: cancellation propagates bidirectionally and unconditionally (a child abort cancels its parent), with no policy surface at all.

## Decision

1. **Two verbs at node and run level.** `cancel` is cooperative: it is delivered through the liveness/acknowledgement channel, gives the work a grace window to drain, runs `on_cancel` reactions and cleanup, suppresses pending retries, and is idempotent. `kill` is immediate: the work stops now, no node-level reactions fire (the loop-level terminal reaction for the resulting outcome still fires once — it rides terminal truth). Cancel escalates to kill only by explicit request or an author-declared grace bound — never silently.
2. **Cancellation is delivered where liveness already flows.** In-flight long work learns of cancel/pause as a side effect of proving it is alive; work that never reports is subject to kill as the fallback. No second control channel.
3. **Parent-close policy on spawned work.** Sub-loop and agent-spawning nodes declare what happens to their children when the parent closes: `terminate` (default), `cancel` (cooperative drain), or `abandon` (fire-and-forget — the child continues independently). Propagation is strictly parent → child; a child's failure or cancellation never cancels the parent implicitly.
4. **Typed failure boundary for spawned work.** A sub-loop failure crosses into the parent as a classified failure (stable class + reference), fail-closed: unknown or oversized detail is redacted to the class. This is what makes error routes usable on sub-loop nodes — an unroutable string is not a contract.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Single stop verb (today)

- **Description**: Only hard stop exists.
- **Pros**: Simple.
- **Cons**: No cleanup, no notification on stop, no graceful drain of minutes-long agent work; every stop loses in-flight context.
- **Why rejected**: Incompatible with reactions (ADR-006) and long-running-first (ADR-004).

### Alternative 2: Bidirectional propagation (Mastra)

- **Description**: Parent and child cancellation propagate both ways.
- **Pros**: Nothing outlives anything unexpectedly.
- **Cons**: A child's local abort tearing down a days-long parent is exactly the accidental blast radius an agent OS cannot afford; removes the fire-and-forget pattern entirely.
- **Why rejected**: Direction and policy must be explicit; the default is containment, not contagion.

### Alternative 3: Cancel with automatic timed escalation to kill by default

- **Description**: Every cancel silently becomes kill after a fixed grace.
- **Pros**: Guarantees termination.
- **Cons**: A silent default deadline on cleanup contradicts the no-default-time-limits posture (ADR-004); cleanup of long work may legitimately take a while.
- **Why rejected**: Escalation exists but is explicit or author-declared, never an invisible default.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Operators and agents get a safe verb (cancel: notify, clean up, drain) and a decisive one (kill), with predictable child behavior including fire-and-forget.
- Sub-loop failures become routable, closing the gap that would otherwise make error routes useless on composite nodes.

### Negative

- Cooperative cancel depends on the work acknowledging liveness; fully silent work only responds to kill (accepted — that is what kill is for).

### Risks

- The grace semantics must be identical across run-level and node-level cancel, and cancel/kill/timeout must collapse into one idempotent cancellation path per SD-001 — the techspec restates this against the session-supervision machinery.
