# ADR-005: Network Usage Is Visible at Release; Configurable Spend Limits Are Deferred

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-07-11

## Context

Live network participation spends model tokens on message-triggered activations. Market evidence is unambiguous that opaque, multiplying agent spend destroys trust (the Cursor pricing incident is the canonical case) and that a real-time usage meter plus spend caps have become table stakes in agentic products. With all three modes public in one release (ADR-004), Live-mode spend exists from day one of the new model.

Two cost-control layers exist and must not be conflated:

1. **Bounded activation by design** — every live participation carries finite activation bounds (how much a message-triggered wake may do before further messages simply accumulate). This is part of the participation contract itself, not a user-managed budget.
2. **User-managed spend limits** — operator-configured ceilings (per workspace, per period) with alerting, in the style of monthly caps.

## Decision

1. **Usage visibility ships in the release**: network-attributed usage is observable in near-real-time, attributable per run, per conversation space, and per workspace, and is truthful — actual provider-reported usage when available, explicitly marked unavailable when not, never estimated-and-presented-as-actual.
2. **Bounded activation remains a contract property** of Live mode (finite by design, exhaustion visible with its reason), independent of any user-managed budget.
3. **Configurable spend limits, alerts, and period caps are deferred** to a later cost-management effort and are an explicit non-goal of this PRD.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Meter + configurable limits in the release

- **Description**: Ship visibility and operator-configured ceilings together.
- **Pros**: Matches the table-stakes bar; strongest trust posture at launch.
- **Cons**: Grows an already-maximal release (ADR-004) with a policy system (scopes, periods, exhaustion semantics, alerting) that deserves its own design.
- **Why rejected**: Operator decision — transparency now, control system later.

### Alternative 2: Neither meter nor limits (ship, measure later)

- **Description**: Release live participation with bounded activation only; add observability afterward.
- **Pros**: Smallest scope.
- **Cons**: Live spend with no visibility repeats the documented trust-destroying pattern; even bounded activation is opaque without a meter.
- **Why rejected**: Unacceptable trust risk for a spend-bearing feature.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Users can always answer "what did the network cost me, where" — spend is never silent.
- The release scope stays honest: visibility is a projection of data the activation contract already produces, while the limits policy system waits for its own effort.

### Negative

- Until the follow-up ships, an operator who wants a hard monthly ceiling on network spend cannot set one; their guardrails are per-participation activation bounds plus visibility.
- The deferred effort inherits an expectation debt: the meter's existence will make "where do I cap this" the first user question.

### Risks

- "Deferred" drifting to "never" while Live adoption grows → the PRD records configurable limits as the named next cost-management effort, and the usage meter's design must not preclude attaching limits to the same scopes it reports.

## Implementation Notes

Usage attribution scopes (run, conversation space, workspace) should mirror the scopes a future limits system would police, so the follow-up composes instead of re-modeling.
